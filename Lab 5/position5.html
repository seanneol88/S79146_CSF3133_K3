The position Property

Five values, five different behaviours. Scroll this page slowly to see the sticky bar stick and the fixed chip stay put in the corner.

1. static — the default

position: static with top: 40px and left: 40px

The offsets are written but ignored: an element that is static simply sits where the normal flow puts it.

2. relative — nudged from its own place

A normal block, sitting still.
position: relative; top: 12px; left: 32px
The block after it has not moved.

The box is shifted 12 pixels down and 32 to the right of where it would have been, but the space it originally occupied is still reserved — which is why the third block did not slide up.

3. absolute — pinned to a positioned parent

Badge

This grey panel is position: relative. Its child is position: absolute; top: 8px; right: 10px, so the badge lands in the panel's top-right corner and the panel behaves as if the badge were not there.

Remove position: relative from the panel and the badge would jump to the top-right corner of the page instead, because the nearest positioned ancestor would become the viewport.

4. fixed — pinned to the window

position: fixed element → the chip in the bottom-right

A fixed element is measured from the viewport, not the document, so it stays in the corner while everything else scrolls underneath it. That is why it is used for chat buttons and cookie bars — and why it can cover content on a small screen.

5. sticky — relative until it is needed

Scroll down inside this page:

I stick to the top of the window

position: sticky; top: 0; keeps the bar in the normal flow, then fixes it to the top of the viewport once you scroll past it. It stops being sticky when its parent scrolls away.
