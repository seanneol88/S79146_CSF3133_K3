Float and Clear

1. float left and float right

float: left; width: 46%
float: right; width: 46%
This box follows, after both floats

A floated element is taken out of the normal flow and pushed to one side. Text and inline content wrap around it, but block boxes further down the page start at the top of the float unless they are cleared.

2. A wrapper that is NOT cleared

float: left
float: right

The red dashed border is the wrapper, and it has collapsed to almost nothing: the two floats escaped it, so the wrapper thinks it contains no content at all.

3. The same wrapper, cleared

float: left
float: right

Same markup, one extra rule: .wrap-clear::after gets content: "", display: table and clear: both. A zero-height element is generated after the floats, the wrapper has to grow to contain it, and the green border now hugs both boxes.

4. Three ways to clear

  • clear: left; on the next element pushes it below any left float.
  • clear: right; does the same for a right float, and clear: both; for either side.
  • overflow: hidden; on the wrapper works too, because it starts a new block formatting context.

The ::after clearfix in section 3 is the safest of the three: it does not cut off content that overflows the wrapper, and it does not need an extra empty element in the markup.
