About the University

A campus on the South China Sea

Universiti Malaysia Terengganu, usually shortened to UMT, is a public university on the coast of Kuala Nerus in Terengganu, Malaysia. Its campus sits beside the South China Sea, which makes the water itself part of the teaching: students walk from a lecture hall to a jetty, a hatchery or a reef survey within the same afternoon. That setting shaped the subjects the university is best known for, among them marine science, oceanography, fisheries and the management of coastal resources.

The institution's history reaches back to 1972, when fisheries and marine science teaching began in Kuala Terengganu. It grew into Kolej Universiti Sains dan Teknologi Malaysia, or KUSTEM, in 1999, and was upgraded to a full university under its present name in 2007. Alongside the marine disciplines, UMT now teaches ocean engineering, informatics, business, economics and social development, so a degree there can lead to a laboratory, a research vessel, a government agency or a company in the blue economy.

Teaching is built around fieldwork. Undergraduate projects often take place on the islands and estuaries near the campus, where students sample water, count coral or record catches with local fishing communities. The results feed into research on biodiversity, aquaculture and climate, and they keep the university's work tied to the people whose lives depend on the sea.

Milestones

A short history of the university
Year Milestone
1972 Fisheries and marine science teaching begins in Kuala Terengganu.
1999 KUSTEM, Kolej Universiti Sains dan Teknologi Malaysia, is established.
2007 The college is upgraded and renamed Universiti Malaysia Terengganu.
2010s Marine, ocean engineering and informatics programmes expand on the Kuala Nerus campus.
